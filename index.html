Sidelight

A payment that the route carrying it cannot recognise as one payment.

Lightning payments travel through intermediaries, and under the hash-locked contracts in use today every one of those intermediaries sees the same payment hash. Any two of them can compare notes and prove they carried the same payment. Do that across enough of the network and the route reassembles, along with who paid whom.

Point time-locked contracts replace the shared hash with a curve point, and the sender blinds that point differently for every hop. Each intermediary is offered a value nothing else on the route shares, and the payment still settles because each hop can derive its own secret from the next one's. Everything here runs in this tab, on real secp256k1 arithmetic. No server, no node, no wallet.

1 · Build a route and send the same payment twice

One sender, one recipient, and intermediaries in between. The identical payment is constructed both ways so the two schemes can be compared on equal terms.

2 · What each hop is shown

The same route, the same payment, the two schemes side by side. One column repeats; the other does not.

3 · Two hops compare notes

Pick any two intermediaries and let them collude. They exchange exactly what their own channel showed them, which is all a real node ever has.

4 · It still has to pay

Unlinkability is worthless if the money stops moving. The secret is walked back along the route and every hop's own lock is opened by the scalar it derives, checked here against the point it was given.

What is actually being trusted

secp256k1, and the sender to pick its blinding offsets at random. Nothing else. No intermediary has to be honest for the privacy to hold, because none of them is given anything worth comparing. The recipient's secret never appears on the route at all, and the hop nearest the recipient learns a scalar that is not it.

Implemented: per-hop blinded payment points, the backwards settlement walk with each hop's scalar checked against its own point, and the hash-locked comparison it is measured against. Not implemented: adaptor signatures over real channel commitments, onion routing and the delivery of offsets to each hop, timeouts and failure paths, and anything touching a network. Schnorr signatures are a precondition for PTLCs on Bitcoin and are not modelled here. This page proves the unlinkability construction; it moves no money.
